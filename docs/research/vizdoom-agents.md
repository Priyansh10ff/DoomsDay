# Prior ViZDoom agents: observations, action spaces, frame skip, algorithm, compute

Date: 2026-11-25. Author: `research` subagent. Feeds ADRs on observation design, action space and frame skip, entity set encoder, and RL algorithm.

How sources were read, and limits:
- arXiv PDFs could not be parsed by the fetch tool, so papers were read through the ar5iv HTML rendering (`ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org`), queried through a small extraction model that returned quotes. I did not read the pages end to end myself.
- The hide-and-seek, Deep Sets and OpenAI Five pages were read only for their first 100,000 characters.
- The rendering sometimes doubles digits (for example "7000" shown as "70007000"). Affected numbers are flagged.
- Venues are reported only where the arXiv abstract page I opened states them; otherwise "venue UNVERIFIED".
- **The PPO paper (arXiv 1707.06347) could not be opened** (the ar5iv page was empty). No PPO primary source backs the algorithm rows; PPO settings below come from papers that used PPO.
- No fetched page contained instructions aimed at the agent.

Target hardware for "reachable?" verdicts: 24 CPU threads, 15.4 GB RAM, one RTX 5060 laptop GPU.

## 1. Summary

- **All three reference agents are pixel agents.** Arnold (108x60 RGB), DFP (84x84 or 128x128 grayscale), Sample Factory (128x72 RGB). None uses engine object/sector telemetry as the policy input at test time. I opened no ViZDoom paper with a telemetry-only policy; the closest primary evidence for entity-centric observations is OpenAI's hide-and-seek agent (not Doom). This is a gap: the organizers' recommended observation style is under-evidenced in the Doom literature.
- **Privileged engine information helped as a training-only signal.** Arnold used an "enemy present in frame" label from the engine as an auxiliary prediction target, not at test time, and reported best K/D rising from under 2.0 to over 4.0.
- **Frame skip 4 is the consistent default**; skip 2 was used for Sample Factory's duel/deathmatch. The ViZDoom platform paper tested many skips and found 4 to 10 the best band on one simple scenario.
- **Action spaces moved from flat enumerations of button combinations (8 to 256 actions) to factorized multi-discrete heads** (Sample Factory: 7 independent heads, 12,096 joint actions). Sample Factory's full space has an optional USE head, which our task needs for doors.
- **Compute is the main risk.** Sample Factory's headline results used 4x10^9 frames (battle) and 2.5x10^9 frames per agent (duel/deathmatch) on 36-core servers. DFP used about 50M steps. Arnold took up to a week. A 10-core/20-thread workstation with one GTX 1080 Ti and 128 GB RAM reached about 59.5K FPS with pixel rendering in Sample Factory; a trainer written during a hackathon should not be assumed to match that.
- **Two rule conflicts found.** (a) Arnold selects between its navigation and action networks with a fixed condition ("no enemies detected or no ammo"), which is a hand-written rule in the decision path (rule 4.1). (b) ViZDoom's objects and sectors info cover the whole level, so they must be filtered to respect the no-god-view rule; and depth/labels buffers "contain noise" if the `viz_nocheat` flag is on, which matters if the organizers' eval harness sets it (open item).

## 3. Reusable hyperparameters, architecture details, compute

### Arnold (Lample and Chaplot; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1609.05521)
- Input: 108x60 RGB, single frame, recurrent.
- Optimizer RMSProp; minibatch 32; replay 1,000,000 frames; gamma 0.99; epsilon 1 -> 0.1 linearly over the first million steps; update every 4 steps; sequence updates with minimum history 4 and updates on 5 states.
- Frame skip 4. Navigation net actions: move forward, turn left, turn right.
- Auxiliary head: FC 512 -> k game features (enemy-present indicator used).
- Learning rate, conv sizes, LSTM width, GPU: not found.
- Compute: up to one week. Not reachable.

### DFP (Dosovitskiy and Koltun; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.01779)
- Perception: conv 8x8/32/stride 4, 4x4/64/stride 2, 3x3/64/stride 1, FC 512. Measurement and goal modules: three FC layers of 128 each. Prediction module split into expectation and action streams, 512 hidden each ("we build on the ideas of Wang et al."). Leaky ReLU with slope 0.2.
- Predicted temporal offsets: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 steps; only the last three weighted in the objective, coefficients (0.5, 0.5, 1.0).
- Adam, lr 1e-4 decayed, beta1 0.95, beta2 0.999, eps 1e-4; batch 64 sampled after every 64 new experiences; experience memory 20,000 steps; 8 parallel actors (garbled digit, medium confidence).
- Budget: 51.2M steps (basic), 2,000,000 iterations (large net). About 70M steps/day on the authors' setup.
- Reachable in scale. It is a supervised-prediction method with epsilon-greedy action selection, not a policy-gradient method.

### Sample Factory APPO (Petrenko et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.11751 ; docs https://www.samplefactory.dev/09-environment-integrations/vizdoom/)
- Table A.5: lr 1e-4; Adam beta1 0.9, beta2 0.999, eps 1e-6; grad-norm clip 4.0; rollout T=32; batch 2048 samples; 1 epoch; V-trace rho-bar = c-bar = 1; PPO clip range [1/1.1, 1.1]; entropy coefficient 0.003; critic loss coefficient 0.5; gamma 0.99 (skip 4) or 0.995 (skip 2).
- Network: conv 8x8/4 (32), 4x4/2 (64), 3x3/2 (128), FC 512, GRU 512; game-info FC 128.
- Sampler sizing: 16 to 32 envs per core; "more or less optimal training setup for a 10-core machine": `--num_workers=20 --num_envs_per_worker=20 --batch_size=2048 --env_frameskip=4 --use_rnn=True`.
- Policy lag reported: 5 to 10 SGD steps on average.
- PBT (population 8, mutate bottom 70% every 5x10^6 frames, 15% mutation rate, factor 1.2) was used for duel/deathmatch. Not reachable on one laptop.

### Hide-and-seek entity encoder (Baker et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.07528)
- Entity embedding 128 (dense, shared per entity type), residual self-attention with 4 heads of size 32, masked average pooling, MLP 256, LSTM 256.
- PPO + GAE: lr 3e-4, clip 0.2, grad clip 5, entropy 0.01, gamma 0.998, lambda 0.95, BPTT truncation 10.
- Their scale (31.7 billion frames, minibatch 64,000) is irrelevant to us; the layer sizes are small.

### Notes for our machine (my inference, not from a source)
- Sample Factory's 59.5K FPS figure was on 128 GB RAM with 640 env instances. With 15.4 GB RAM, the number of concurrent ViZDoom instances may be limited by memory before CPU. Measure per-instance memory early.
- I found no published FPS figure for telemetry-only ViZDoom extraction. This needs a local benchmark.

## 4. Recommendations for advisor

1. **Treat multi-discrete factorized heads with frame skip 4 (and an explicit USE head) as the evidence-backed default action design, and consider skip 2 only if aim precision proves limiting.** Rests on the Sample Factory action-space rows (paper Table A.4 and repo file), the Arnold, DFP and ViZDoom-paper frame-skip rows, and the Sample Factory skip-2 row. Strength: strong and consistent for frame skip 4 across four independent sources; strong for factorization at full-button scale (one peer-reviewed source plus its code); weak for doors specifically, since none of the opened papers evaluated door/keycard levels. That skip 2 helps aiming is my reading of where it was used, not a quoted finding.
2. **Budget the algorithm choice around roughly 10^8 to 5x10^8 frames and treat sampler throughput as a first-class engineering deliverable; let a local FPS benchmark decide between synchronous PPO and asynchronous V-trace-style training.** Rests on the Sample Factory throughput and budget rows, the IMPALA rows and the DFP throughput row. Strength: strong that published headline results need 10^9+ frames and server hardware; moderate that asynchrony about doubles throughput (cross-framework comparison); weak on what a from-scratch trainer with 15.4 GB RAM will achieve (no published datapoint, must be measured). No PPO primary source was opened.
3. **For the observation and entity encoder, the best-supported template is rays plus a line-of-sight-masked entity set (shared per-entity embedding, attention or pooling) into a recurrent core, with engine telemetry also used as training-only auxiliary prediction targets.** Rests on the hide-and-seek and Deep Sets rows (architecture), the Arnold game-features row and DFP measurement-prediction rows (auxiliary targets), and the ViZDoom docs row (objects/sectors are whole-level and must be filtered; `viz_nocheat` risk). Strength: strong for the encoder pattern in general RL but **no Doom-specific evidence** for telemetry-only policies (a real gap); strong for auxiliary targets in Doom (two Doom papers, large effects); the `viz_nocheat` question should go to the organizers before the observation ADR is final.
