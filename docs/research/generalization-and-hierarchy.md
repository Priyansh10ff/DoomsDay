# Generalization to unseen levels, and hierarchy for long-horizon tasks

Date: 2026-11-25. Author: `research` subagent. Shorter file by request; feeds curriculum and hierarchy ADRs. **This is the thinnest of the four files**: fewer sources, no dedicated domain-randomization paper opened, and no ViZDoom procedural-generation paper opened.

How sources were read, and limits:
- arXiv PDFs could not be parsed, so papers were read through the ar5iv HTML rendering, queried through a small extraction model that returned quotes. I did not read the pages end to end myself.
- The rendering sometimes doubles digits; one value (Option-Critic's termination margin) was illegible and is omitted.
- Venues are given only where the arXiv abstract page states them.
- No fetched page contained instructions aimed at the agent.

Target hardware for "reachable?" verdicts: 24 CPU threads, 15.4 GB RAM, one RTX 5060 laptop GPU.

## 1. Summary

- **The number of distinct training levels is the main lever for zero-shot generalization.** Procgen: agents "strongly overfit to small training sets"; closing the train-test gap needed up to 10,000 levels, and the benchmark default is 500 levels. On this evidence a handful of hand-made maps is too few.
- **Doom-specific evidence agrees in direction.** DFP trained on a single fixed scenario collapsed on another (33.6 to 1.6 frags), while training on a randomized-texture, harder variant transferred to all test settings. Arnold used 10 training maps, 3 test maps and texture randomization. Neural Map lost about 12 points going from its training map to unseen maps.
- **Level sampling can be improved cheaply.** Prioritized Level Replay changes only which level is sampled next and improved Procgen test returns (about 28% over uniform sampling by its table; 76% when combined with a data-augmentation method) at 25M steps on 200 levels.
- **Hierarchical RL evidence is weaker and older.** FeUdal Networks beat an LSTM baseline on Montezuma's Revenge and DMLab memory tasks, but not on every Atari game, at an A3C budget of hundreds of millions of observations. Option-Critic learns options end to end but its options tend to shrink without a regularizer. Neither paper tests a key-then-door task in a 3D game. Arnold's two-network split helped on test maps but its gate is hand-written (rule 4.1).
- **Rules note:** procedural maps, ACS scripts and randomization are training-only tools and are allowed (rule 4.2). Any hierarchy must have a learned manager; a scripted switch between sub-policies is forbidden (rule 4.1).

## 3. Reusable hyperparameters, architecture details, compute

- **Procgen PPO** (Cobbe et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.01588): lr 5e-4, gamma 0.999, lambda 0.95, entropy 0.01, clip 0.2, 256-step rollouts, 64 envs per worker, 8 minibatches, 3 epochs; 500 training levels; 25M (easy) or 200M (hard) steps; 200M steps cost about 24 GPU-hours and 60 CPU-hours.
- **PLR** (Jiang et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.03934): level score from the L1 value loss of the last rollout on that level; rank-based prioritization with temperature beta 0.1; staleness mixing coefficient rho 0.1 (0.3 on MiniGrid). No extra model.
- **FeUdal Networks** (Vezhnevets et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1703.01161): goal embedding 16; manager horizon c=10 through a dilated LSTM (r=10); worker reward = extrinsic + alpha x cosine-similarity intrinsic term; A3C; lr sampled log-uniform in 10^-4.5 to 10^-3.5; discounts 0.99 (worker) and 0.999 (manager) on Montezuma. Budget: hundreds of epochs of 1M observations.
- **Option-Critic** (Bacon et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1609.05140): 2 to 8 options; termination margin regularizer (value not legible); entropy regularizer on intra-option policies.
- **Doom precedents**: DFP 90/10 texture split; Arnold 10 train / 3 test maps with texture randomization.

## 4. Recommendations for advisor

1. **Plan for hundreds or more procedurally varied training maps with a fixed held-out set, not a small hand-built set.** Rests on the Procgen overfitting rows, the DFP transfer row, and the Arnold and Neural Map rows. Strength: strong and consistent in direction across a 2D benchmark and three Doom papers; the specific numbers (500, 10,000) come from 2D arcade-style games and are order-of-magnitude guides only. Tooling for generating valid Doom maps with keys and doors was **not researched** here (gap).
2. **Consider value-loss-prioritized level sampling (PLR-style) as the curriculum mechanism over generated maps.** Rests on the PLR row. Strength: one source, clear gains at a reachable 25M-step budget, no added networks; venue unverified, and not tested in 3D or Doom in what I read.
3. **Treat full hierarchical RL (FeUdal, Option-Critic) as optional and higher risk; if hierarchy is wanted for the innovation score, the better-evidenced forms are a learned gate over a small number of sub-policies or a slow goal-setting head over the spatial memory, and the gate must be learned.** Rests on the FeUdal rows (wins and losses), the Option-Critic row, the Arnold split row and the Active Neural SLAM row. Strength: moderate that temporal abstraction helps on memory and sparse-reward tasks at large budgets; weak at hackathon budgets; no opened source shows a learned hierarchy solving key-then-door tasks in a 3D shooter.
