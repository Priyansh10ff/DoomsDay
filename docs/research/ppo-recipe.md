## 1. Summary

- **PPO's own Atari settings are small and close to ADR 0004.** 8 actors, T=128, 3 epochs, minibatch 32x8, Adam 2.5e-4 annealed linearly to 0, clip 0.1 annealed to 0, gamma 0.99, lambda 0.95, value coefficient 1, entropy 0.01, 40M frames. The paper's continuous-control ablation has clip 0.2 slightly ahead of 0.1 and 0.3 (0.82 vs 0.76 and 0.70 normalized score).
- **Implementation details move results as much as the algorithm, but the measured evidence is feedforward MuJoCo.** Engstrom et al.: the code-level optimizations were worth 421 to 557 reward on three MuJoCo tasks against 99 to 242 for the PPO-vs-TRPO difference; reward scaling, learning-rate annealing and orthogonal initialization were the ones that mattered among the four ablated; value clipping was not. Andrychowicz et al. (250,000+ agents): small last-layer policy initialization and observation normalization matter; value-loss clipping and Huber loss hurt; per-minibatch advantage normalization and gradient clipping have little effect; learning-rate decay is secondary; entropy bonus gave no benefit in continuous control. **None of this was measured on recurrent, discrete, 3D-game agents.**
- **Recurrent handling: ADR 0004's T=64 is mid-range overall, but longer than the PPO-family recurrent systems opened.** PPO-family: hide-and-seek 10, OpenAI Five 16, Sample Factory 32, DD-PPO up to 128. A3C/IMPALA-family: UNREAL 20, MERLIN 20, Mirowski 50 or 75, IMPALA 100. OpenAI Five reports that staleness and higher sample reuse hurt in their recurrent PPO. R2D2's burn-in evidence could not be opened.
- **Timeouts.** Pardo et al. (ICML 2018): if the time limit is only a training device, bootstrap from the value at the timeout; if the limit is part of the task, give the agent the remaining time as an input. Andrychowicz et al. found special handling unnecessary at the large limits they used. ADR 0004 already follows the first rule; the second case matters if the judged episodes have a time limit.
- **Set encoders.** The hide-and-seek sizes are already in `vizdoom-agents.md`. OpenAI Five uses a "Process Set" module for unordered sets and running-statistics observation normalization clipped to (-5, 5); the per-unit pooling operator was not confirmed. Deep Sets sizes and AlphaStar: not opened.
- **Auxiliary losses have the strongest directly relevant evidence in this file.** Mirowski et al.: agents with a coarse 4x16 depth-prediction auxiliary target had roughly double the learning-curve area of an LSTM A3C baseline on 3D mazes (for example 104.3 vs 44.3 on Static 1), with two confounds noted in the table, and depth as a prediction target beat depth as an input. UNREAL: mean human-normalised Labyrinth score 54% to 87%, about 10x faster to reach A3C's best. Both at budgets (10^8 environment steps, 16 to 32 workers) at the edge of reach.

## 3. Reusable hyperparameters, architecture details, compute

### PPO (Schulman et al.; https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347, read via reader proxy)
- Atari: 8 actors, T 128, 3 epochs, minibatch 32x8, Adam 2.5e-4 linearly annealed to 0, clip 0.1 annealed to 0, gamma 0.99, lambda 0.95, value coefficient 1, entropy 0.01, 40M frames.
- MuJoCo: T 2048, 10 epochs, minibatch 64, Adam 3e-4, tanh MLP 64-64, policy and value not sharing parameters, no entropy bonus, 1M steps. Clip for these runs: not in the extracted text (UNVERIFIED; the ablation's best was 0.2).
- Loss: L_CLIP - c1 L_VF + c2 S.
- Compute: 40M Atari frames with 8 actors. **In reach** in scale.

### Implementation details with measured effect (Engstrom et al. https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.12729 ; Andrychowicz et al. https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.05990)
- Helped: reward scaling/normalization, Adam lr annealing, orthogonal init with a small-gain policy output layer (Andrychowicz: 100x smaller last layer), observation normalization with clipping (study default 10; OpenAI Five uses 5).
- Hurt or no benefit: PPO value-loss clipping, Huber value loss.
- Second-order: per-minibatch advantage normalization, gradient clipping (0.5 default), linear lr decay, Adam vs RMSprop.
- Tune first: learning rate (3e-4 "safe default"), discount, clip (start 0.25 per Andrychowicz, 0.2 best in the PPO paper's ablation), lambda (0.9 recommended by Andrychowicz, 0.95 in PPO).
- Reference-code extras reported by the ICLR blog post: Adam epsilon 1e-5; init gains sqrt 2 hidden, 0.01 policy head, 1 value head; log clip fraction and approximate KL.
- Compute: both studies are 1M to 2M-step MuJoCo runs, feedforward. Reproducing any single setting is in reach; their sweeps (80+ agents per cell, 250,000 agents) are not, and are not needed.

### Recurrent handling
- Zero initial state, reset at episode end, store the rollout's initial state, train on whole sequences (reference-code practice per the ICLR blog post).
- Unroll lengths in use: 10 (hide-and-seek), 16 (OpenAI Five), 20 (UNREAL, MERLIN), 32 (Sample Factory), 50 or 75 (Mirowski), 100 (IMPALA), up to 128 (DD-PPO).
- Reuse: OpenAI Five lists sample reuse of 1.0 and 1.1 and measured losses from more reuse; DD-PPO used 2 epochs; Sample Factory 1 epoch; PPO Atari 3 (feedforward).
- Burn-in: UNVERIFIED (R2D2 not opened).

### Timeouts (Pardo et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1712.00378)
- Training-only limit: bootstrap with gamma x v(s') at the timeout; never at a true termination.
- Task-defined limit: add remaining time, normalized to [-1, 1], to the observation.
- PPO runs used the Baselines implementation with the PPO paper's hyperparameters, 10 seeds.

### Set encoders
- Hide-and-seek: see `vizdoom-agents.md` (128-d embeddings, 4 heads x 32, masked average pool, LSTM 256).
- OpenAI Five: "Process Set" module for unordered sets; running mean/std normalization clipped to (-5, 5); LSTM 4096 (not reachable, and not needed).
- Deep Sets sizes and AlphaStar: not opened in this pass.

### Auxiliary losses
- Mirowski et al. (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.03673): conv 8x8/4 (16), 4x4/2 (32), then FC or LSTM 256; Nav A3C stacks LSTM 64 (or 128) and LSTM 256, with velocity, previous action and reward as extra inputs; depth target 4x16, 8 classes; depth weights 1 to 33; loop-closure weight 1 to 10; RMSProp; lr in [1e-4, 5e-4]; entropy in [1e-4, 1e-3]; unroll 50 or 75; 16 workers; up to 10^8 environment steps (2.5x10^7 agent steps at action repeat 4); 64 hyperparameter samples per architecture. One run is at the edge of **reach** (2.5x10^7 decisions is within the ADR 0004 plan); the 64-run sweep is not.
- UNREAL (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.05397): conv 8x8/4 (16), 4x4/2 (32), FC 256, LSTM 256; 32 threads; unroll 20; lr log-uniform 1e-4 to 5e-3; entropy 5e-4 to 1e-2; replay 2k steps; action repeat 4; 84x84 RGB. Total frames and hardware not found. Reach: the agent is small; the frame budget is unknown.

### Where the evidence differs from ADR 0004 (listed, not decided)
- Gradient-norm clip: ADR 1.0; PPO code and Andrychowicz default 0.5; Sample Factory 4.0; hide-and-seek 5. Reported as second-order.
- Rollout / BPTT length: ADR 64; PPO-family recurrent systems 10 to 32, DD-PPO up to 128.
- Epochs: ADR 3 (same as PPO Atari, feedforward); recurrent large-scale systems used 1 to 2 and OpenAI Five measured a cost of reuse.
- Value-target normalization: ADR on; Andrychowicz found it helps on some tasks and hurts on others.
- Value clipping: ADR silent; two studies say do not use it.
- Clip-range annealing: ADR constant 0.2; PPO Atari anneals 0.1 to 0. No ablation opened.
- Policy-head init gain and Adam epsilon: ADR silent; small-gain policy head has a large measured effect in continuous control.

## 4. Recommendations for advisor

1. **Keep ADR 0004's core settings and add the cheap details that have measured support: orthogonal init with a small-gain output layer on every policy head, fixed-range or running normalization of vector observations, reward scaling, linear lr decay, and no value-loss clipping.** Rests on the PPO Table 5 and Table 1 rows, the two Engstrom rows and the Andrychowicz initialization, normalization and value-clipping rows. Strength: strong and consistent across two large studies (one ICLR 2020, one unverified venue), but **all of it is feedforward MuJoCo at 1M to 2M steps**; nothing was measured on a recurrent, multi-head, discrete agent in a 3D game. The entropy and advantage-normalization findings should not be read as applying to us.
2. **Treat rollout length and epoch count as the two recurrent-specific settings to test early (ADR 0004's T = 64 with 3 epochs is longer and reuses more than the PPO-family recurrent systems opened, apart from DD-PPO's rollout), and keep the bootstrap-on-timeout rule.** Rests on the BPTT-length row, the OpenAI Five staleness row, the DD-PPO row and the two Pardo rows. Strength: strong that timeout bootstrapping is right when the limit is a training device (ICML 2018); moderate that reuse and staleness hurt recurrent PPO (one source, measured at a scale thousands of times ours); **no source opened compares sequence lengths**, and the burn-in evidence (R2D2) is UNVERIFIED. If the judged episodes turn out to have a time limit, Pardo's time-aware case becomes relevant.
3. **Give the pixel branch and the recurrent state a coarse depth-prediction auxiliary loss (our rays as the target, predicted from the recurrent state), and rank loop-closure and pixel-reconstruction losses below it.** Rests on the three Mirowski depth rows, the loop-closure row and the UNREAL rows, with the Arnold and DFP auxiliary-target rows in `vizdoom-agents.md` as Doom-specific support. Strength: large effects in 3D mazes at a budget near ours (AUC roughly doubled; depth as target better than depth as input), from one paper whose final venue I could not confirm and with two unresolved confounds (architecture change, asterisked baselines); it is not Doom evidence and not PPO evidence (A3C). It is still the best-supported route found to make the pixel branch usable if `viz_nocheat` removes depth at evaluation.
