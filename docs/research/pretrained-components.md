# Pretrained, non-Doom perception components: frozen image encoders and monocular depth

Date: 2026-11-25. Author: `research` subagent (second pass). Feeds the pixel-branch decisions in ADR 0003 and ADR 0006 (the `viz_nocheat` fallback).

How sources were read, and limits:
- Papers were read through the ar5iv or arXiv HTML rendering, queried through a small extraction model. I did not read the pages end to end myself. Pages over 100,000 characters were read only for their first 100,000 unless stated.
- Several key results exist only as figures; where the text gave no number I say so instead of estimating.
- Venues are given only where an arXiv abstract page or the official repository I opened states them.
- Not opened: R3M, MVP and DINOv2 original papers (their results here come only from the comparison papers that tested them); arXiv 2403.12039 (PointNav with monocular depth estimators); the Bochum unsupervised-features ViZDoom paper; Geo-Nav (AAAI 2022). These appear only as UNVERIFIED rows or not at all.
- No fetched page contained instructions aimed at the agent.

**Plain statement of the limits.** None of the frozen-encoder or depth-estimation results below was measured on Doom. I found one ViZDoom paper with a frozen pretrained encoder; its encoder was pretrained on video (the part I read does not say whether in-domain or out-of-domain), and it reports no numeric scores and no from-scratch baseline in that part. So there is **no Doom evidence for or against a non-Doom pretrained encoder**. Doom's sprite-and-texture rendering at 60x80 is far from the photographs and robot videos these models were trained on (my observation); no source opened tests that gap.

Target for "in reach?" verdicts: CPU-only PyTorch, 24 threads, 15.4 GB RAM, plan of about 10^3 decisions/s in training, `screen` at 60x80, real-time play at 8.75 decisions/s (ADR 0006).

## 1. Summary

- **Against a small CNN trained from scratch with augmentation, frozen pretrained encoders showed no consistent gain.** Hansen et al. is the only opened study that makes exactly this comparison, including with PPO: the from-scratch baseline "performs comparably to the frozen pre-trained visual representations on most tasks" (manipulation, figure only, no numbers in text), and behaviour-cloning numbers are mixed in both directions. Schneider et al. (NeurIPS 2024) found the same for model-based RL, including out-of-distribution tests and one navigation-style task.
- **The positive results come from settings unlike ours.** Parisi et al. (ICML 2022): frozen encoders beat from-scratch perception, but with behaviour cloning, not RL. VC-1: frozen ViT-L reaches 70.3% ImageNav success with RL, but the comparison is a frozen or fine-tuned randomly initialized ViT-B at 500M steps, not a small CNN, and the model is a 307M-parameter transformer. PIE-G: a frozen ImageNet ResNet18 cut at layer 2 improved visual generalization (about +55% on its hardest distractor benchmark) over from-scratch methods with augmentation, with off-policy DrQ-v2 on continuous control at 84x84.
- **Two findings recur and are cheap to respect:** early-layer features serve control better than final-layer features (PIE-G layer 2 vs layer 4: 884 vs 306 on Walker Walk; Parisi et al. agree for fine control and disagree for navigation), and in-domain pretraining beats ImageNet pretraining for navigation encoders (DD-PPO, quote only).
- **Monocular depth as a substitute sensor: at the published setting (518x518, batch 1) it is too slow for the training loop, and it is unproven as an RL input at our scale.** Depth Anything V2 Small has 24.8M parameters under Apache-2.0 (larger sizes are non-commercial) and outputs relative inverse depth, not metric depth. The only latency figures opened are on GPUs: 13.3 ms per 518x518 image on an RTX 4080; 50 ms for four images on a Jetson Orin with TensorRT. No CPU figure was verified, and no figure at low resolution was found. In the one RL comparison opened (RVN-Bench, DD-PPO on 8 GPUs), adding predicted depth to RGB clearly helped and ground-truth depth helped about 10% more.
- **Rules.** A pretrained backbone raises a question under rule 4.3 (core agent built during the event) that only the organizers can settle; `spatial-memory.md` already noted that a pretrained backbone "sits badly" with that rule. A training-only alternative with direct evidence is to use engine depth as an auxiliary target for our own small CNN (see `ppo-recipe.md`, Mirowski rows).

## 3. Reusable hyperparameters, architecture details, compute

### From-scratch pixel baselines that matched frozen encoders (Hansen et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05749)
- PPO: six conv layers, 32 channels, kernels 7, 5, 3, 3, 3, 3, stride 2, ReLU, then LayerNorm + Linear; 224x224 RGB plus proprioception; random shift with padding 10 (reported as not necessary for PPO).
- DrQ-v2: four conv layers, 32 channels, 3x3, first stride 2; stacks of 84x84 frames.
- Behaviour cloning: five conv layers, 32 channels, 3x3 stride 2, BatchNorm + ReLU; random shift padding 12.
- Compute: not stated for PPO. The networks are small; 224x224 input is not what we render.

### PIE-G frozen stem (Yuan et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.08860)
- ImageNet ResNet18, features from the end of layer 2, flattened, one trainable FC layer; encoder frozen; BatchNorm running statistics left updating; 84x84 input, 3 stacked frames; DrQ-v2; 500k interaction steps; one RTX 3090.
- Reach: the paper gives no parameter count or CPU latency for the truncated stem; needs a local measurement. The training budget is small by our standards. The method is off-policy.

### VC-1 (Majumdar et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.18240)
- ViT-L, 307M parameters, masked auto-encoding on 4,000+ hours of egocentric video plus ImageNet; "over 10,000 GPU hours" to train. ImageNav policy: DD-PPO, 500M steps.
- Reach: **not reachable** on CPU for training-time inference (my judgement from model size; no latency figure extracted).

### Depth Anything V2 Small (https://github.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2 ; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.09414)
- 24.8M parameters, DINOv2 small encoder, Apache-2.0, default input 518, relative inverse depth.
- Latency: 13.3 ms at 518x518 on RTX4080 12GB (ONNX, CUDA); four images in 50 ms on Jetson Orin AGX (TensorRT). CPU: UNVERIFIED.
- Reach (my arithmetic): about 75 images/s at the published GPU setting is roughly 7% of the planned 1000 decisions/s, before any CPU penalty. Real-time evaluation at 8.75 decisions/s allows about 114 ms per decision, which the GPU figures fit and a CPU may or may not.

### RVN-Bench predicted-depth agent (https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03953)
- DD-PPO, ResNet-50 encoder, 2-layer LSTM, RGB plus Depth Anything V2 depth; 8 L40S GPUs. Not reachable; cited for the direction of the effect only.

### Rules notes (my reading; for the advisor and organizers)
- Rule 4.3 says the core agent architecture and training pipeline must be built during the event and allows pre-existing libraries. Whether pretrained weights count as a library or as pre-existing agent parts is not stated in `docs/TRACK_BRIEF.md`. Open question for the organizers.
- Rule 4.2: a pretrained network is not an engine mod, so it does not conflict with vanilla evaluation, but it must ship with the inference setup and run within whatever time limit the harness has (unknown).
- Rule 4.1 is not affected in my reading: a depth network produces an observation feature, not an action.

## 4. Recommendations for advisor

1. **Do not make a frozen non-Doom image encoder the default pixel branch; a small CNN trained by us is supported as no worse, and it avoids the rules question.** Rests on the two Hansen rows (the only direct small-CNN-vs-frozen comparison, including PPO), the Schneider row (NeurIPS 2024, negative including out-of-distribution and a navigation-style task) and the VC-1 row (gains only for a 307M-parameter model against a ViT baseline). Strength: moderate. The comparisons agree, but they are manipulation, locomotion and model-based RL; the PPO result is a figure caption without numbers; **nothing is Doom**. The counter-evidence (Parisi et al.) is imitation learning.
2. **If an ablation with a pretrained stem is wanted for the generalization story, the evidenced form is PIE-G's: the first two stages of an ImageNet ResNet18, frozen, behind a trainable layer; ask the organizers about rule 4.3 first and measure its CPU cost before committing.** Rests on the two PIE-G rows and the Parisi layer finding. Strength: strong numbers for visual generalization (returns roughly 1.5 to 2 times SVEA on several distractor tasks; layer 2 far better than layer 4), from one paper with unverified venue, with an off-policy algorithm on continuous control. No evidence for PPO, for 3D navigation, or for 60x80 Doom frames. No CPU latency exists in the sources.
3. **Do not plan on monocular depth estimation as a substitute sensor in training; prefer engine depth as a training-only auxiliary target for our own pixel encoder, and treat a depth model at most as a late, measured experiment.** Rests on the Depth Anything latency rows (GPU only, 518x518 batch 1; CPU UNVERIFIED), the relative-depth row, the RVN-Bench row and the teacher-student row, plus the Mirowski depth-as-target rows in `ppo-recipe.md`. Strength: strong that the Small model is permissively licensed; the "too slow for a 1000-decisions/s loop" verdict is my arithmetic from GPU figures at one resolution and would need re-measuring at low resolution; moderate that predicted depth helps RL navigation in photorealistic scenes (one 2026 preprint, 8 GPUs) and that policies trained on true depth do not transfer to predicted depth without matching training; **no evidence at all on Doom imagery**, where the depth model itself is untested.
