# Exploration: ICM, RND, Episodic Curiosity, NGU, Go-Explore

Date: 2026-11-25. Author: `research` subagent. Feeds the exploration ADR (and informs reward shaping).

How sources were read, and limits:
- arXiv PDFs could not be parsed, so papers were read through the ar5iv HTML rendering, queried through a small extraction model that returned quotes. I did not read the pages end to end myself.
- NGU and both Go-Explore papers were read only for their first 100,000 characters (appendices partly unread).
- The rendering doubles some digits (for example "20M" appears as "2020M", "18" as "1818"). Where a number could be affected it is flagged. One headline number (the EC speed-up over ICM) could not be resolved and is marked UNVERIFIED.
- Venues are given only where the arXiv abstract page states them.
- No fetched page contained instructions aimed at the agent.

Target hardware for "reachable?" verdicts: 24 CPU threads, 15.4 GB RAM, one RTX 5060 laptop GPU.

## 1. Summary

- **ICM and Episodic Curiosity (EC) both have direct ViZDoom evidence** on the sparse-reward `MyWayHome` maze. ICM + A3C reached 100% success where A3C alone reached 0% (sparse spawn). EC matched ICM's final performance on ViZDoom and converged faster (factor unverified, most likely 2x). On DMLab at a 20M-step budget EC clearly beat both PPO and ICM on sparse tasks.
- **ICM has a failure mode that matters for a shooter.** In the EC paper's DMLab experiments the ICM agent "learns to entertain itself by firing until it runs out of ammunition", and covered less ground than plain PPO with no reward. A prediction-error bonus can be farmed by actions with hard-to-predict effects.
- **RND is the simplest to implement and is built to avoid the stochasticity trap**, but its published results rest on 128 to 1024 parallel environments and about 2 billion frames, and its authors say it handles local, not long-horizon global, exploration. It also showed a "dancing with skulls" habit of seeking dangerous objects.
- **EC costs about 1.84x PPO wall time** in the paper, with a ResNet-18 siamese reachability network on pixels. It is practical but is the most engineering of the three small-scale options.
- **NGU and Go-Explore are not reachable as systems.** NGU: 256 actors, 35 billion frames. Go-Explore's main variant explores with random actions (no network) from restored simulator states, then distills over billions of frames. One NGU idea is cheap in isolation: an episodic k-nearest-neighbour pseudo-count bonus over a memory that starts empty each episode.
- **Episodic versus lifelong novelty (my inference, not measured in any source opened):** a lifelong bonus (RND, ICM) decays as training proceeds and leaves no explicit signal at test time; an episodic bonus (EC, NGU's episodic part) rewards covering new ground within each episode, which is the behaviour wanted on an unseen map. No opened paper tests this on unseen Doom maps or with telemetry observations.
- **Rules note:** all of these are training-time reward terms and do not touch the action path, so they are compatible with rule 4.1. Go-Explore's exploration phase depends on simulator state restore (training-only, allowed by 4.2), but that phase is not a neural policy; only its distilled network could be submitted.

## 3. Reusable hyperparameters, architecture details, compute

### ICM (Pathak et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.05363)
- Encoder: 4 conv layers, 32 filters, 3x3, stride 2, padding 1, ELU; feature dim 288; policy LSTM 256.
- beta = 0.2 (forward vs inverse loss weight), lambda = 0.1 (policy loss weight), lr 1e-3, 20 A3C workers, action repeat 4 in training ("we sample the policy without any action repeat during inference").
- Overhead: 1.09x PPO (measured in the EC paper).
- Reachable. Risk: firing/ammo farming.

### RND (Burda et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.12894)
- PPO: rollout 128, 128 envs, 4 minibatches, 4 epochs, entropy 0.001, lr 1e-4, clip range [0.9, 1.1], lambda 0.95, Adam.
- Reward mix: 2 x extrinsic + 1 x intrinsic; gamma_E 0.999, gamma_I 0.99; two value heads V_E and V_I; intrinsic non-episodic; obs whitening with clip [-5, 5]; intrinsic reward normalized by running std of intrinsic returns; predictor updated on 25% of samples.
- Reachable to implement; published results are at about 2x10^9 frames.

### Episodic Curiosity (Savinov et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.02274)
- Bonus b = alpha x (beta - C(M, e)), C = 90th-percentile comparator score against memory; alpha 1 (ViZDoom), beta 0.5, memory 200, novelty threshold 0, k about 5.
- R-network: siamese ResNet-18 branches (512 outputs) plus FC comparator (four hidden layers of 512 per the supplement); offline from random policy (300K 4-repeated steps for ViZDoom, Adam lr 1e-4, batch 64, 50K iterations) or online (retrain every 720K 4-repeated steps, 10 epochs). Online was better on DMLab.
- PPO: lr 2.5e-4, entropy 0.01, task reward scale 5 (ViZDoom). DMLab budget 20M 4-repeated steps.
- Overhead 1.84x PPO. Reachable.

### NGU (Badia et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.06038)
- Episodic bonus about 1/sqrt(pseudo-count), counts from a kernel over the k=10 nearest neighbours of an inverse-dynamics embedding; memory empty at episode start; multiplied by min{max{alpha_t, 1}, 5}; beta max 0.3.
- Full system (R2D2, 256 actors, 35B frames): not reachable. Episodic k-NN bonus alone: cheap to compute, no stand-alone evidence.

### Go-Explore (Ecoffet et al.; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.10995 and https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.12919)
- Archive of cells; select, return (restore), explore for 100 random-action frames; cells 11x8 downscaled frames or domain-knowledge tuples.
- Not reachable end to end; not aligned with zero-shot evaluation.

## 4. Recommendations for advisor

1. **The evidence favours an episodic novelty bonus (memory emptied each episode) over a purely lifelong one as the main exploration signal, with the cost and the missing key/door evidence stated in the ADR.** Rests on the EC DMLab row (20M steps), the EC ViZDoom row, the EC transfer row and the NGU mechanism row. Strength: strong that EC beats PPO and ICM on sparse 3D navigation at a reachable 20M-step budget (peer-reviewed, DMLab); on ViZDoom it only matched ICM's final score. The claim that episodic bonuses carry over better to unseen maps is my mechanism-based inference, **not measured**. Counter-evidence to record: on the one keys-and-doors task (dense reward) EC did not beat PPO, and EC measured 1.84x slower than PPO.
2. **If a prediction-error bonus is used, the evidence leans to RND-style over ICM-style for an agent that can shoot, using RND's normalization recipe and its two value heads.** Rests on the ICM firing row, the RND mechanism, coefficients and limitations rows. Strength: strong that ICM was farmed by firing in DMLab (peer-reviewed measurement); RND's immunity to that is by construction and my inference, not a measured Doom result; weak on RND at our scale (all results at 128+ envs and about 2x10^9 frames; authors say it does not solve long-horizon global exploration). ICM is still the only one of the two with a positive ViZDoom result.
3. **Do not plan on NGU or Go-Explore as systems; keep only the detachment/derailment diagnosis as a checklist for analysing failed runs.** Rests on the NGU compute row and the Go-Explore phase-1, budget and policy-based rows. Strength: strong and unambiguous on compute (35B frames; 4.35B to 10B frames for robustification) and on the rules point that Go-Explore's exploration phase is not a neural policy. Whether a small goal-conditioned "return" policy is useful at our scale is **not evidenced** in what I read.
